# Rendering: CPU compositor instead of wgpu

**Decision.** `scrust-render` composites the stage on the CPU (resvg/tiny-skia for SVG and pen strokes, a hand-written sampler for sprites and graphic effects) and the player presents the result with softbuffer (native) or a 2D canvas (web). PRODUCT.md §13 asks for a wgpu renderer; that is not implemented.

**Why.** Collision detection has to be answered on the CPU with the same sampling rules as drawing (§13: "GPU 読み戻しはしない"), so a CPU sampler is needed in any case. Using it for presentation as well gives one code path for native and wasm, identical pixels for `touching color` and the screen, and no GPU or wasm-bindgen dependency. The cost is fill rate: full-screen sprites at high scale are much slower than on a GPU.

**What follows scratch-render.** The model transform (`Drawable._calculateTransform`), AABB (`Rectangle.initFromModelMatrix`), fencing (`getFencedPositionOfDrawable`, 15 px fence), tight bounds from opaque pixel centres, candidate/intersection scan for `touching sprite`, colour matching with the 5/5/4-bit masks and compositing over white for `touching color`, effect formulas (`sprite.frag` / `EffectTransform`), SVG MIP levels at powers of two capped at 2048 px, the pen's half-pixel offset for 1 and 3 px lines.

**Verified against the reference.** `conformance/render` is a project that logs renderer-dependent results (fenced positions for upright and rotated sprites, `set size` clamping, `touching sprite` at 0-2 px distances, rotated and scaled, ghosted and hidden, `touching edge`, `if on edge, bounce`, `touching color` including the 5/5/4-bit tolerance). The golden values in `conformance/render/expected.json` were recorded from scratch-vm 5.0.300 + scratch-render 2.2.84 running with WebGL in a browser (`conformance/out/rendertest/index.html`); `node conformance/render/compare.js` checks scrust against them: 78/78 values identical, positions bit for bit.

Getting there required reproducing three things that are easy to miss:

- scratch-render stores positions, scales and matrices in `Float32Array`s. Bounds, and therefore positions after `if on edge, bounce`, are float32 values (`218.36251831054688`). `Xf` rounds through `f32` at the same places.
- Silhouettes are sampled at `floor(u * (width - 1))`, without rejecting coordinates outside `[0, 1]`, and rotated/scaled SVG skins use a 2x2 "linear" test. A sprite therefore extends about one texel beyond its quad.
- Collision scan regions are AABBs snapped outwards to integers, and `sin(180 deg)` is `1.22e-16` rather than 0, so an edge at an exact integer lands just past it and the region grows by a pixel. Together with the previous point this is why two sprites one pixel apart are "touching" in Scratch.

**Still approximate.** Scan regions always use the quad's AABB (scratch-render switches to tighter hull bounds for drawables whose hull has been computed). (Silhouette MIPs, SVG sizing, rounded positions and texture filtering now follow TurboWarp's scratch-render; see [turbowarp.md](turbowarp.md).) Text bubbles use approximate metrics and the system (or embedded Noto Sans) font instead of Scratch's fonts and are drawn above all sprites; pen lines are anti-aliased by tiny-skia. No pixel comparison of the rendered frame itself has been made.

## Metal (macOS)

**Decision.** On macOS, drawing moves to the GPU through Metal (`crates/scrust-render/src/gpu.rs`, bindings from `objc2-metal`): sprites with all graphic effects, the stage, the pen layer (lines and stamps are rendered into a GPU texture) and the text overlays (bubbles, monitors, the ask box, rasterised on the CPU as before and uploaded as textures). Both the player (`SoftRenderer`) and the GUI's editor scene (`Scene`) use it. Without a Metal device, on other platforms (wasm has its own backend, see below), with `SCRUST_RENDERER=cpu`, and in `--deterministic` runs (unless `SCRUST_RENDERER=metal`), the CPU compositor draws as before.

**What stays on the CPU.** Collision queries keep sampling the CPU silhouettes, as scratch-render does (§13), so `touching` results do not depend on the GPU. `touching color` needs the pen layer: it is read back from the GPU when it changed since the last read.

**How it matches the CPU path.** Vertices come from the same model transform (`Xf`, already rounded through f32; see below), the fragment shader ports `Effects::point` and `Effects::color`, the same SVG MIP level is uploaded, sampling is nearest for distorted or exactly 1:1 drawables and bilinear (clamped to the edge) otherwise, and blending is premultiplied "over" in gamma space. Pen lines use scratch-render's line shader (see turbowarp.md), with the half-pixel offset for 1 and 3 px pens.

**Measured.** The demo project rendered at 960x720 after 90 deterministic frames: 98.8 % of the pixels identical to the CPU frame, the rest anti-aliasing differences of at most 32/255, same game state. `conformance/render`: 78/78 with either backend. A pen test (lines of 1-9 px, a stamp, a `touching color` scan of the stage) found 194 of 195 hits in common; the two differing probes sit on anti-aliased line edges. Speed (editor scene, 960x720, rotated SVG sprites with effects): 20 sprites 89.6 ms -> 1.2 ms per frame, 200 sprites 661 ms -> 3.1 ms.

**Geometry as WebGL draws it.** Where a pixel centre falls exactly on a texel boundary (a resolution-2 bitmap at an odd half-size, stamped at an integer position) or on a quad edge, which texel NEAREST picks and whether the pixel is covered depend on float32 rounding in the vertex stage and on the rasteriser's edge rules. Image scanners read such stamps back with `touching color`, so these choices end up in project state. scrust reproduces them on the same GPU: the six vertices of scratch-render's quad (`_createGeometry`, two triangles in its order, texcoords as vertex data) are computed on the CPU as its vertex shader does, `u_projectionMatrix * u_modelMatrix * vec4(a_position, 0, 1)` in float32 (`tw_clip`). Stamps use `penStamp`'s viewport (the fast bounds, scaled and snapped) and `ortho` over it. ANGLE keeps a GL framebuffer's row 0 as the Metal texture's row 0: the pen texture is top down because scratch-render's projection flips it, while the stage target is bottom up like WebGL's drawing buffer and is flipped when read back or presented. Skin textures are sampled clamp-to-edge (`CLAMP_TO_EDGE`), because a covered edge pixel may sample just outside [0, 1]. Coordinate-encoding probe costumes (stamped and drawn, upright, rotated, scaled, resolution 1 and 2) give the same texel at every pixel as Chrome.

**Pen operations share one render pass.** Every pen operation queued since the last frame (lines, stamps, after a clear that becomes the pass's load action) is encoded into a single pass over the pen texture. A pass per operation loads and stores the whole texture each time; a project stamping ~1 200 times per frame (`HOUSE.sb3`, a splat renderer built from `stamp`) took 142 ms per frame to draw that way and takes 3-5 ms in one pass.

**Presentation.** The native window presents from the GPU: the frame is drawn into the window's `CAMetalLayer` drawable and committed without waiting, so the GPU finishes it while the next step runs (the CPU waits for the previous frame only before it rewrites overlay textures). Without Metal the window presents through softbuffer as before. The embedded host (`--embed`, the GUI) still reads each frame back and hands the RGBA to the GUI through shared memory (see `gui.md`).

## WebGL2 (browser)

**Decision.** In wasm32 builds the same compositor interface is implemented with WebGL2 (`crates/scrust-render/src/webgl.rs`): the browser GUI's interpreter and programs built with `--target wasm` draw sprites, effects, the pen layer and overlays on the GPU instead of compositing on the CPU in WebAssembly, which was most of a frame's cost (Animdustry, pen stamps: 24 ms -> 0.5 ms per frame; Raycaster, pen lines: 12.6 ms -> 0.8 ms; sprite-only projects about 4x).

**No bindings.** The module records each frame as a stream of `f32` commands (begin, sprite quad, overlay, pen layer, pen lines in batches, pen stamp, clear) and makes one import call (`scrust_gl.gl_run`) per frame; the page's glue (`crates/scrust-render/web/gl.js`, shared by `gui/web/bridge.js` and inlined into `crates/scrust-player/web/index.html`) replays it. Skin rasters are uploaded once per (skin, size) as on Metal; `touching color` reads the pen layer back with `readPixels` when it changed. Without WebGL2 (or without the glue, as in Node) `gl_init` returns 0 and the CPU compositor draws as before.

**Geometry.** The shaders, vertex math (`tw_clip`, `penStamp`'s viewport) and sampling are the Metal backend's without its ANGLE emulation: in a browser this is scratch-render's own GL. The stage is drawn straight into the canvas's default framebuffer and the pen layer into a framebuffer stored top down, as scratch-render does. Against the CPU compositor at scale 2 frames are identical up to anti-aliasing (0.01 % of pixels); at scale 1, resolution-2 bitmaps put pixel centres exactly on texel boundaries and NEAREST picks the texel Chrome picks (about 1 px shifts against the CPU, which does not model this). `conformance/render`: 78/78 with WebGL.

**Presentation.** The canvas is the frame: the standalone page draws into its stage canvas, and the GUI copies the program's WebGL canvas onto the stage with `drawImage`, without reading pixels back. The editor's scene (`crates/scrust-scene-wasm`) stays on the CPU: it redraws only when the editor changes, and its frames are read back as RGBA.

**Costumes are decoded at start-up.** `SoftRenderer::new` decodes every costume on all cores and uploads bitmaps to the GPU before the first frame, as Scratch loads all assets before running. Decoding on first use stalled each frame that showed new costumes (320-575 ms for the first frame of `HOUSE.sb3`, 4 096 PNGs). Sampled textures use shared storage on unified-memory GPUs.
